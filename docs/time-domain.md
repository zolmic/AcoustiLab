# Time-domain outputs and rational models

This note covers spec Sections 3 ("Time domain and plant export", "Impulse
responses", frequency grid policy), 10 ("Time-domain outputs"; phase,
excess phase and group delay; the poles, zeros and Q table) and 16 ("From
response to impulse response", phase modes), with errata E46 and E47. The
code is `crates/acoustilab/src/time/`, the tests are
`crates/acoustilab/tests/time.rs`, and the independent references are
generated by `tools/time/numerics_refs.py` and `tools/time/ir_refs.py`
(numpy), with fixtures in `crates/acoustilab/tests/data/time_*.json`.

Conventions follow `docs/conventions.md`: `e^{+jωt}`, RMS phasors. The
time-domain responses are those of the netlist's stated drive (`meta.drive`),
exactly as a solve reports them.

## The uniform re-solve

Bins are f_k = k·fs/N, k = 0..N/2 (default fs = 48 kHz, N = 8192; N any power
of two from 256 to 65 536, fs from 1 to 768 kHz; the wasm export caps N at
16 384). Every bin of the solved band is a direct solve of the network at
that frequency, with the drive applied as in `Circuit::solve`. It is never
an interpolation of the log grid. By default the band starts at the first
bin and ends at the netlist sweep's maximum or Nyquist, whichever is lower
(`f_min_Hz`, `f_max_Hz` override both).

**DC.** The network is often singular at 0 Hz, so DC is never solved. Its
value comes from the model's own low-frequency asymptote, probed by two
extra solves at f_a = fs/(1024·N) and 2·f_a (5.7 and 11.4 mHz at the
defaults). A first-bin rule is not enough: the design template's pad leak
works against the driver's suspension compliance with a corner of 0.19 Hz,
a 0.8 s time constant, far below the 5.86 Hz bins, which look flat. With n
the log-log slope between the two probes:

| slope | rule | DC value |
|---|---|---|
| n > 1/2 | `zero`: the response falls towards DC (a leak) | 0 |
| \|n\| ≤ 1/2 | `extrapolated`: flat (a sealed pressure chamber) | Re(2·H(f_a) − H(2f_a)) |
| n < −1/2 | `singular`: grows towards DC (the impedance of a compliance) | the same, with a warning that the IR does not decay |

m = round(n) is the order of the zero (m > 0) or pole at DC. The corner
f_c = f_a·(|H(f₁)|/|H(f_a)|)^(1/m), clamped to [f_a, f₁], is where that
asymptote meets the level at the lowest bin. Both are reported
(`extrapolation.dc_order`, `dc_corner_Hz`) and used by the minimum-phase
step. If a probe solve fails, the two lowest solved bins stand in for the
probes.

**Above the solved band.** The response continues with the model's
asymptote, taken from the top 1/6 octave of the band: |H| ∝ f^n, with n
clamped to [−4, 1] (a passive network cannot rise faster than 6 dB/octave
asymptotically; a steeper local slope at the band edge comes from a nearby
resonance), and phase continued linearly (constant group delay). The
continuation is multiplied by a half-cosine taper,
½·(1 + cos(π·(f − f_hi)/(fs/2 − f_hi))), which is 1 at the band top and 0 at
Nyquist. When the band reaches Nyquist there is no taper, and the Nyquist
bin is the real part of the solved value. DC and Nyquist are real, and the
full spectrum is Hermitian.

## Impulse, step and energy-time curve

h[n] = IDFT(H) is the response to a one-sample unit pulse of the drive:
the probe's unit per unit of drive per sample, h[n] ≈ h(n/fs)/fs. The
returned buffers are rotated so that the first `pre` samples (default N/32,
5.3 ms) hold negative times. Sample m is at t = t0 + m/fs with
t0 = −pre/fs. No other shift is applied unless delay alignment is
requested (below).

* **Step**: the running sum of the rotated IR, the response to a unit step
  of the drive. It settles at H(0).
* **Energy-time curve**: 10·log10|h_a|² re its maximum, where h_a is the
  analytic signal (spectrum doubled at positive frequencies, zeroed at
  negative ones), floored at −300 dB.
* **Late energy**: the energy in the second half of the circular buffer
  relative to the total. That half holds negative times, so it collects
  any non-causal response and whatever of the causal tail has not decayed
  by N/(2·fs).
* **Band-edge energy** (report field `band_edge_energy_dB`): the share of
  the spectrum's energy above the solved band. It sets the mixed-phase
  IR's pre-response (see "Causality").
* **Delay alignment** (Section 16, mode 2): with `align_delay` the
  mixed-phase spectrum is multiplied by e^{+jωτ}, τ being the pure-delay
  estimate below, and `delay_removed_s` states it.

### Checks

* The IRs of an RC high-pass (100 Hz) and a series-RLC low-pass (1 kHz,
  Q = 5), solved to Nyquist, match numpy's irfft of their analytic spectra
  built by the same rules to 1e-12 of the peak. Parseval holds to 1e-12.
* The low-pass IR against its continuous analytic response, time-aliased
  with period N/fs: they differ only by the spectrum outside the band. The
  bound on that (the sum of |H| over the out-of-band bins plus the Nyquist
  and DC terms, 1.737e-3) holds and is reached at t = 0, where every
  out-of-band term has the same sign. Beyond 1 ms they agree to 1e-4 of the
  peak.
* The step settles at H(0); the peak lies at the analytic
  tan(ω_d t) = ω_d/σ to within a sample.

## Impulse length (erratum E46)

A pole's envelope falls by 80 dB in ln(10⁴)/|Re a| = (4·ln 10/π)·Q/f
= 2.93·Q/f. The causal half of the buffer must hold that:
N/(2·fs) ≥ 2.93·Q/f for every resonant pole. E46 rounds the factor to 2.9.
On a 100 Hz, Q = 20 resonator (`tools/time/ir_refs.py`, numpy) the late
energy is:

| causal half N/(2·fs) | late energy |
|---|---|
| 1.1·Q/f (a buffer of one T60) | −30.0 dB |
| 2.2·Q/f (T60) | −60.1 dB |
| 2.9·Q/f (E46 as printed) | −79.1 dB |
| N = 16 384 (0.17 s) | −23.2 dB |
| N = 32 768 (0.34 s) | −46.5 dB |
| N = 65 536 (0.68 s) | −92.8 dB |

The engine reproduces these to 0.05 dB. `ir_length_check` takes the resonant
poles of a vector fit (next sections) and reports the lowest-frequency one,
the binding one (largest 2.93·Q/f), whether N covers it, and the smallest
power-of-two N ≥ 4096 that would (for this resonator 65 536, beyond the
16 384 audition limit). It covers resonances. A real pole below the fitted
band, such as the design's 0.19 Hz leak corner, is seen instead by the DC
probe (`dc_corner_Hz`, time constant 1/(2π·f_c)) and by the measured late
energy.

## Causality (erratum E47)

Section 17 asks the IR "from the full-band complex response" to have
pre-response energy below −80 dB. For the mixed-phase IR that holds only
when the response is negligible above the solved band. The taper, and
Nyquist itself, band-limit the response with a zero-phase window, which
spreads the impulse symmetrically in time however long N is:

| network (fs 48 kHz, band to 20 kHz, N = 8192) | level at 20 kHz | mixed-phase late energy | minimum-phase filter |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 Hz high-pass (flat to the band edge) | 0 dB | −15.0 dB | −89.4 dB |
| 200 Hz notch (flat at the band edge) | 0 dB | −15.0 dB | −159.9 dB |
| 1 kHz resonator, Q = 5 | −52 dB | −61.1 dB | −227 dB |
| 100 Hz resonator, Q = 5 (N = 16 384) | −92 dB | below −80 dB | below −80 dB |
| design template, p_drp (N = 8192) | rising | −29.1 dB | −36.2 dB (the 0.8 s leak tail) |

The minimum-phase filter is causal by construction (next section), so the
−80 dB test belongs to it. For mixed phase the report gives the band-edge
energy alongside the late energy. The design template has −25.2 dB of its
spectral energy above 20 kHz, because the simulator's response rises into
its 20 kHz resonance. Its filters also carry the 0.8 s leak decay, which no
audition-length buffer holds (see E46 above).

## Minimum phase

Two products, both from the real cepstrum:

* **Analysis counterpart** (`min_phase`): the continuous-time
  minimum-phase function with the model's magnitude, meaning the solved
  band continued by the model's asymptote without taper. The excess phase
  and the decision are measured against it.
* **Minimum-phase filter** (`min_phase_fir`, report field `ir_min_phase`):
  the discrete minimum-phase sequence with the magnitude actually served
  on the grid, taper included (Section 16, mode 1). It is causal. Its phase
  differs from the analysis counterpart near the band edge, because the
  taper's zero at Nyquist adds about a sample of delay (0.9° at 1 kHz at
  48 kHz).

### Method

A discrete cepstrum approximates the continuous Hilbert transform only
well below its Nyquist frequency, and a zero at DC (every leaky headphone)
puts a log singularity on the grid. `tools/time/minphase_study.py` is an
independent numpy implementation of the method and of simpler variants on
analytic spectra served as the engine serves them. It gives the largest
phase error over 20 Hz–20 kHz: a plain cepstrum of the served magnitude
errs 159° on a 100 Hz high-pass, 81° on a 1 kHz resonator and 56° on a
notch. The steps that remove those errors:

1. **Analytic factors.** H = D(s)^m·(1 + s/ω_t)^n·X with D(s) = s/(s + ω_c),
   m and f_c = ω_c/2π from the DC probe, ω_t = 2π·f_hi and n the
   asymptotic exponent. Both factors are minimum-phase with known phase.
   The remainder X is finite at DC and flat above f_hi. X(0) is taken from
   the probe, H(f_a)/D(f_a)^m. Taking it from the lowest bin instead costs
   1.4° wherever X still varies below that bin. The filter divides out
   D^m only. Dividing D out alone brings the high-pass to 0.35°.
2. **Refinement** (`refine`, default 4; the "generous zero padding"): |X| on
   a grid four times finer, from X's impulse response zero-padded to 4·N
   samples, split at N/2 so that negative times stay negative. The original
   bins keep their exact values. Refinement resolves features a few bins
   wide: a Q = 20 notch at 200 Hz errs 1.45° without it and 0.0015° with
   it, and the high-pass (whose X varies across the lowest bins) 0.35° and
   0.0016°. Padding X rather than H matters. With a 0.2 Hz leak corner,
   H's own response is far longer than N and its zero-padded spectrum is
   wrong near DC (12.7° instead of 1.0°).
3. **Extension** (`extend`, default 8, analysis only): the grid continues to
   8·fs/2 with X flat there, so the discrete (cot-kernel) Hilbert transform
   approaches the continuous one over the band. For the resonator, still
   falling 12 dB/octave at 20 kHz, the error is 57° with no extension,
   3.7° at ×4, 0.83° at ×8 and 0.09° at ×16.
4. Real cepstrum of ln|X| (floored at 1e-15 of its peak), folded (c₀, 2c_n
   for 0 < n < M/2, c_{M/2}), exponentiated, and the analytic factors
   multiplied back at the original bins. The cepstrum has 32·N points for
   the analysis counterpart and 4·N for the filter. Both spectra keep |H|
   exactly. The filter's energy at negative times is −89 dB for the
   high-pass and below −120 dB for the others, against −15 dB and −61 dB
   for their mixed-phase IRs.

A band solved up to Nyquist is continued from the bin below it. The
Nyquist bin holds only Re H, and taking its magnitude as the asymptote
cost 0.35° with an all-pass factor present.

**Polarity.** The excess phase is referred to the all-pass with unit DC
gain: when it is nearer ±π than 0 at the lowest solved bin, the
counterpart's sign is inverted and `polarity` reads −1.

### Accuracy (tests `minimum_phase_matches_analytic_phase`, `excess_phase_of_an_all_pass_factor`)

Band solved to 20 kHz and tapered above, fs = 48 kHz, N = 8192; the phase of
the analysis counterpart against the analytic phase of each minimum-phase
network:

| network | observed | asserted |
|---|---|---|
| RC high-pass, 100 Hz (DC zero) | 0.0016° over 20 Hz–20 kHz | 0.01° |
| notch 200 Hz, Q_z = 20, Q_p = 2 | 0.0015° | 0.01° |
| series-RLC low-pass 1 kHz, Q = 5 (−12 dB/octave at the edge) | 0.042° to 1 kHz, 0.83° to 20 kHz | 0.05°, 1° |
| ladder with a 0.2 Hz corner and a 1 kHz, Q = 2 low-pass | 5–200 Hz under 0.01°, 0.05° to 1 kHz, 1° to 20 kHz | 0.01°, 0.06°, 1.2° |
| notch × all-pass (a − s)/(a + s), a = 2π·500: excess phase against −2·atan(ω/a) | 0.002° | 0.01°; excess group delay to 1e-3 |

The error that remains is set by the magnitude assumed above the solved
band, which the model does not define. The minimum phase at frequency f
depends on the whole magnitude curve. A continuation exponent n
contributes a group delay of about −n/(π·f_hi) at low frequency (±16 µs
per unit of n at 20 kHz). For the design template the analysis counterpart
and the vector fit's zeros (whose model has its own behaviour above
20 kHz) put the excess delay at 95 µs and 68 µs respectively. Excess delays
are therefore uncertain by some tens of µs; the 0.5 ms decision is not
affected.

## Excess phase, pure delay and the phase-mode decision

φ_ex = unwrap(arg(H/H_min)), with the excess group delay −dφ_ex/dω taken
by central differences on the uniform grid. The decision (Section 16)
counts the **trusted band**: bins that are solved, unshaded (validity band
0) and within 60 dB of the band's peak magnitude. Minimum phase is the
default when the largest |excess group delay| there is below the threshold,
0.5 ms by default (`threshold_ms`). The spec sets this "below the one to two
millisecond group-delay audibility figures usually attributed to Blauert
and Laws 1978, to be confirmed from that paper before release"; the
figure has not been checked against the paper here. The **pure-delay
estimate** is the smallest excess group delay in the trusted band, clamped
at 0. An all-pass factor's group delay is positive at every frequency, so
this bounds the pure delay from above, and equals it once the all-pass
delay has died out at the top of the band. The decision uses the raw
excess group delay, pure delay included. The largest value with the pure
delay removed is reported as well.

Results:

* An all-pass lattice with RC = 0.5 ms (group delay 1 ms at DC) gives
  mixed phase, with 1 ms found to 1e-3 and no pure delay.
* A 100 mm matched tube gives minimum phase, with a pure delay of
  L/c = 291.5 µs to 2 %; alignment moves its IR peak by that delay.
* The design template's trusted band is 105 Hz to 1.06 kHz (IEC 60318-4
  below 100 Hz; the rear cavity's lumped limit). It has a near-constant
  excess delay of 94.5 µs (front-cavity depth line and simulator), varying
  by under 1 µs, so minimum phase applies.

Robinson's energy-delay theorem (Section 17, "minimum-phase energy
concentration") is checked on a causal low-pass × all-pass: the filter's
partial energies dominate the mixed-phase IR's at every sample.

## Group delay

* **From the rational model** (`PoleFit::group_delay`): −Re(H′(jω)/H(jω))
  with H′ in closed form from the poles and residues. This is the
  Section 10 output ("group delay from the rational fit, not from wrapped
  phase on a coarse grid").
* **From the uniform re-solve** (`group_delay_dense`):
  Re{DFT(n·h[n])/DFT(h[n])}/fs with signed n, the exact derivative of the
  trigonometric interpolant. No unwrapping is involved.

On a 1 kHz, Q = 5 resonator both agree with the closed form, the fit to
1e-6 relative and the dense estimate to 1e-3 of the peak delay below 10 kHz
(the dense one is limited by the band-limited, time-aliased IR). On the
design template, the fit's group delay agrees with a central difference of
exact solves at f(1 ± 1e-5) to 2 µs from 20 Hz to 10 kHz. The dense
estimate needs an IR that fits in N, which the template's 0.8 s leak tail
does not.

## Vector fitting

Relaxed vector fitting (Gustavsen & Semlyen 1999; Gustavsen 2006) with
common poles for several responses (Deschrijver et al. 2008, fast QR
reduction). The model per response is
H(s) = Σ c_n/(s − a_n) + d + s·e, with real poles and complex-conjugate
pairs in the real basis of `vectfit3`.

| option | default | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `order` | 30 | number of states, pairs counting two (the spec's 20 to 80; the wasm export allows 1 to 80) |
| `iterations` | 12 | pole relocations; the best (lowest weighted RMS) iterate is kept |
| `asymptote` | `constant` | `zero` (strictly proper), `constant` (d), `linear` (d + s·e, e.g. an impedance with inductance) |
| `weighting` | `relative` | 1/\|H\| (dB-and-degree error across the dynamic range) or `uniform` |
| starting poles | log-spaced pairs over the band, Re = −Im/100 | Gustavsen & Semlyen 1999, Section 5 |

* **Relocation.** Each step solves the relaxed least-squares problem,
  σ(s)·H(s) ≈ fitted numerator with Re Σ σ(s_k) = K. Its new poles are the
  eigenvalues of Λ − b·c̃ᵀ/d̃, and unstable ones are reflected. A vanishing
  d̃ is replaced by a fixed small value, as `vectfit3` does.
* **Residues and error.** The residues come from linear least squares with
  the poles fixed. The report gives the RMS relative error, the RMS and
  largest dB error, and the largest phase error. Frequencies are
  normalised by the highest fitted ω internally.
* **Zeros.** With D ≠ 0 they are eig(A − B·C/D). For a strictly proper
  model they come from the output-zeroing dynamics
  (I − B(CB)⁻¹C)·A on ker C. D counts as zero below 1e-6 of the peak |H|,
  since a smaller D only adds a zero near −CB/D, far outside the band, and
  swamps A. A failed eigenvalue iteration leaves a note in the report
  instead of failing the fit.
* **State space.** Real (A, B, C, D, E) per response. Passivity is not
  enforced: a fitted impedance can have a small negative real part outside
  the fitted band. Passivity enforcement for plant export is out of scope.

Largest dB and degree error from 20 Hz to 20 kHz against order (24 points
per octave, relative weighting, constant asymptote):

| network | 10 | 16 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `closed_back_rear_vent.json` p_ear (vented cup, mesh, slit leak, L1) | 9.7e-3 dB / 0.06° | 2.2e-3 / 6.8e-3 | 2.1e-4 / 8.8e-4 | 3.4e-7 / 4.8e-6 | 2.0e-8 / 2.1e-7 | 1.6e-8 / 1.4e-7 |
| design template p_drp, IEC 60318-4 (L1) | 0.25 dB / 2.3° | 0.014 / 0.026 | 9.5e-4 / 0.012 | 2.5e-4 / 8.0e-4 | 1.6e-4 / 5.7e-4 | 6.5e-5 / 4.8e-4 |
| design template p_drp, Type 4.3 (48-segment canal) | 12 dB / 38° | 0.10 / 0.18 | 1.5e-3 / 0.044 | 2.7e-4 / 2.3e-3 | 1.5e-4 / 5.6e-4 | 7.0e-5 / 5.0e-4 |
| design template p_drp, level 0 | 0.026 dB / 0.16° | 1.4e-3 / 0.014 | 8.1e-4 / 9.2e-3 | 1.0e-4 / 1.3e-3 | 7.5e-5 / 8.6e-4 | 6.3e-5 / 5.1e-4 |
| design template zin (electrical input impedance) | 1.1e-4 dB / 1.6e-3° | 6.3e-6 / 1.0e-4 | 2.6e-6 / 4.0e-5 | 2.5e-6 / 1.9e-5 | 3.0e-6 / 1.4e-5 | 1.6e-6 / 1.1e-5 |

The irrational thermoviscous elements (√f wall losses, transmission lines,
the simulator's slits) are smooth over the band. Order 20 meets the brief's
0.1 dB and 1° with a wide margin, and order 30 (the default) reaches
3e-4 dB and 0.002°. The error then saturates near 1e-4 dB, a floor set by
the irrational terms. A known order-19 rational function (8 pairs with Q
from 0.8 to 18, 3 real poles, d and s·e) is recovered with poles and
residues to 1e-9 (observed ~1e-12).

## Poles, zeros and Q table; attribution

`fit_probe` fits a probe on the solve's log grid (optionally a sub-band)
and lists every pole with f = |a|/2π, Q = |a|/(−2·Re a) for pairs, the
T60 = 6.91/|Re a|, and a **weight**: the size of the pole's own term at its
resonance relative to the whole model there. An isolated resonance reads
about 0 dB. The nearly cancelling pole-zero pairs that fits of irrational
responses produce read −35 to −90 dB. A pole is **resonant** when it is a
pair with Q ≥ 1, inside the band, with weight ≥ −20 dB (`q_min`,
`weight_min_dB`). Zeros are listed with the half-plane they lie in. Spec
Section 17 asserts minimum phase only after checking the fit's zeros, and
`min_phase_in_band` does exactly that. The design template's fit has one
right-half-plane pair at 18 kHz, part of the fitted form of its propagation
delay.

**Attribution** (`attribute_poles`, report option `attribute`). This is
the brief's preferred method. Each continuous parameter that an enabled
element depends on (directly or through derived parameters) is perturbed
by +1 % (−1 % at an upper bound). The probe is re-solved and re-fitted,
starting from the base poles (4 iterations), and each resonant pole is
matched to the nearest perturbed pole, requiring a mutual nearest match
within 25 %. The logarithmic sensitivities d ln f/d ln p and d ln Q/d ln p
rank the parameters, and the elements that use the top three are named.
Zero-valued parameters are skipped, and each skip is listed with its
reason. A resonance that no parameter moves (all |d ln f/d ln p| < 0.05)
is attributed to the enabled elements that depend on no perturbed
parameter.

Checks:

* An LC resonator gives d ln f/d ln L = d ln f/d ln C = −1/2 and
  d ln Q/d ln R = −1, all to 0.01.
* On the design template, the 936 Hz coupled resonance goes to the
  diaphragm area (+0.99: the air springs scale as Sd²), the moving mass
  (−0.50) and the cup radius (−0.42). The radius sets the front air spring,
  about 45 % of the total stiffness with the rear cavity.
* The 11.57 kHz, Q = 132 pole goes to the front depth (−0.97: the depth
  half-wave).
* A 7.1 kHz resonance that no parameter moves goes to the ear simulator,
  whose values are fixed.

Cost: one solve and one short fit per parameter, 0.45 s for the
template's 16 parameters natively.

## Interfaces

**CLI** (`crates/acoustilab-cli`):

```sh
acoustilab ir design.json [--probe ID] [--fs 48000] [--n 8192] [--phase min|mixed] [--pre N] [--align] \
    [--wav ir.wav] [--wav-raw] [--csv] [--out FILE] [--no-check] [--set NAME=VALUE]
acoustilab poles design.json [--probe ID] [--order 30] [--attribute] [--json]
```

`ir` prints the JSON report, or with `--csv` the columns t_s, ir,
ir_min_phase, step, etc_dB. `--wav` writes the phase that the decision
chose (or `--phase`) as 32-bit IEEE-float WAV, normalised to a peak of 1
unless `--wav-raw`. The gain and time of the first sample go to stderr.
The WAV has a `fmt ` chunk of 18 bytes with tag 3 and a `fact` chunk, and
scipy.io.wavfile reads it back sample for sample. `--pre 0` starts the file
at t = 0.

**WebAssembly** (`crates/acoustilab-wasm/src/time.rs`; each call takes the
netlist, the overrides and an options object, returns JSON, and rejects
unknown option keys with kind `options`):

* `impulse(netlist, overrides, options)`. Options: `probe`, `fs_Hz`, `n`
  (≤ 16 384), `f_min_Hz`, `f_max_Hz`, `pre_samples`, `refine`, `extend`,
  `threshold_ms`, `ir_length_check`, `order`, `align_delay`, `spectrum`.
  The report holds `ir`, `ir_min_phase`, `step`, `step_min_phase`,
  `etc_dB`, `etc_min_phase_dB` (each N samples, from `t0_s` in steps of
  `dt_s`), `late_energy_dB`, `late_energy_min_phase_dB`,
  `band_edge_energy_dB`, `causal_to_80dB` and `peak_s`. Then `decision`
  (`mode`, `max_excess_group_delay_s`, `at_Hz`, `pure_delay_s`, `band_Hz`,
  `threshold_s`, `polarity`, ...), `min_phase` (`dc_order`,
  `dc_corner_Hz`, `hf_slope`, `polarity`, `cepstrum_len`),
  `extrapolation`, `ir_length` (the E46 check), `delay_removed_s`, `drive`,
  `shading`, `warnings` and `parameters`. With `spectrum` it also holds
  `frequencies_Hz`, `spectrum` {re, im}, `excess_phase_deg`,
  `excess_group_delay_s`, `group_delay_s` and `trusted` (N/2 + 1 each).
* `vector_fit(netlist, overrides, options)`. Options: `probe`, `order`
  (≤ 80), `iterations`, `asymptote`, `weighting`, `f_min_Hz`, `f_max_Hz`,
  `q_min`, `weight_min_dB`, `attribute`, `max_parameters` (≤ 64), `step`,
  `fs_Hz`, `n`, `state_space`. The report holds `band_Hz`, `order`, `fit`
  {rms_relative, rms_dB, max_dB, max_deg, iterations, history, flipped},
  `notes`, `poles` [{re_rad_per_s, im_rad_per_s, f_Hz, Q, real, in_band,
  weight_dB, resonant, t60_s}], `zeros` [{…, rhp}], `min_phase_in_band`,
  `frequencies_Hz`, `response`, `fitted`, `group_delay_s`, `ir_length`,
  `attribution` {method, perturbed, skipped, poles: [{pole, f_Hz, Q,
  parameters: [{parameter, dlnf_dlnp, dlnQ_dlnp, elements}], unmatched,
  fixed_elements}]}, `state_space` {A, B, C, D, E} and `drive`.
* `isolation(...)`: see `docs/isolation.md`.

## Open points

* Section 16's "minimum phase forced above the model's declared validity
  frequency" (a hybrid filter) is left to the auralization layer. The
  pieces are here (`min_phase_fir`, the shading).
* SOFA export (Section 10) is not implemented.
* The 0.5 ms threshold is the spec's, unconfirmed against Blauert and Laws
  (1978).
* Dispersion-aware delay estimates (a group delay varying across the band
  from distributed elements) are reported as the lower envelope only.
* Passivity enforcement of the fitted state space is out of scope.
